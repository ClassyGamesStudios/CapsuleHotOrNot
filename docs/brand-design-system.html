Hot or Not

The app's own slate and cyan palette, set in Figtree, built with the same structure as a modern music-streaming player: panels on a dark ground, white pills, and one accent kept for the vote.

Separation without borders

Surfaces are told apart by a one-step lighter fill and the gap of dark ground between them. Cards, inputs and buttons carry no border, glow or drop shadow, which replaces the 1px hairlines and green winner glow of the old templates.

The five rules

1

Lighter means closer

Each surface is one step lighter than the one it sits on. Steps are small, but on a dark ground the eye reads them clearly.

2

The gap is the divider

Panels never touch. They float on bg-base with an 8px gap, and the dark showing through does the job a border would.

3

Nest one step at a time

A card on a panel is bg-highlight on bg-elevated. Same color vanishes, two steps looks like a button. Inside a card, use spacing and weight.

4

Interaction is a fill change

Hover moves the surface up one step and text from subdued to base. Buttons scale to 1.04. The only strong line is the 3px focus ring.

5

An outline means optional

The one bordered element is the outline button, 1px border-essential. A line marks a low-priority control, never a surface.

Applying it

  • New surface: take the parent's token and go one step up the ramp. Stop there.
  • Two items on the same level: separate them with space-2 or more, not a line.
  • Tempted to add a border: raise the fill one step or widen the gap instead.
  • Status: color the text or a small dot. Never outline the container.
Contrast note. Panel edges (bg-elevated on bg-base) are only about 1.1:1, which is fine for large layout regions. Small controls must stand out through their own label, fill or the focus ring. text-subdued on bg-press is about 4:1, so hover states switch text to text-base.

Texture

Most of the interface stays flat. Texture is for the things you can act on: buttons, the card you've picked, and the field you're typing in. It's a soft gradient in a single color with grain over it, a bit like a riso print. You barely notice it at rest, and it comes up when you hover, focus, pick something or scroll it into view.

Three surfaces

  • Cyan for Vote and the round vote button. A cyan base with a deeper cyan patch and light grain.
  • White for the main buttons. White fading into a pale cyan tint. The grain is dark here, because light grain can't show up on white.
  • Slate for the capsule you picked and a field with focus. Slate on slate, kept low so the text on it stays easy to read.

Five rules

1

One hue

The base, the patch and the grain all come from the same color. The one place two or three colors are fine is the promo banner, as long as it stays around a tenth of the page.

2

Things you can act on

Buttons, the picked card and the focused field. Panels, stat tiles, notices, text and artwork stay flat.

3

Quiet until you're near

It sits at 0.2 by default, rises to 0.55 when it scrolls into view, and reaches 1 on hover, keyboard focus or selection.

4

Dark grain on light

On white and other light surfaces the grain multiplies and darkens. On dark and cyan surfaces it overlays.

5

Check the text

Look at the label at full strength. If it's hard to read against the patch, lower that surface's maximum.

Where it goes

  • Use it on: Vote and the icon vote button, the white main buttons, the capsule someone picked, a text field with focus.
  • Leave flat: panels, stat tiles, reward cards, notices, navigation, table rows, artwork, outline and text buttons, and the buttons inside the promo banner, which is already a gradient.
How it moves. The contrast eases over about a third of a second. If someone has reduced motion turned on, it just switches. The live demos use GSAP, with ScrollTrigger for the scroll part. Run npm install once in the app folder so the scripts load. Without them, hover and focus still work in plain CSS, but scrolling won't change anything.

The recipe

/* one number per element, 0 to 1 */
@property --c { syntax: "<number>"; inherits: true; initial-value: 0.2; }

.tx { --c: var(--c-rest); position: relative; isolation: isolate; overflow: hidden; }

/* the patch: one blurred shape in a nearby shade */
.tx::before {
  content: ""; position: absolute; z-index: -1;
  width: 85%; height: 150%; left: 40%; top: -25%; border-radius: 50%;
  background: var(--blob); filter: blur(14px);
  opacity: calc(var(--c) * var(--mx));
  transform: scale(calc(.85 + var(--c) * .3));
}

/* the grain: feTurbulence noise, fractalNoise, baseFrequency .55, 2 octaves, saturate 0 */
.tx::after {
  content: ""; position: absolute; inset: 0; z-index: -1;
  background-image: url("data:image/svg+xml;utf8,...");
  mix-blend-mode: overlay;            /* multiply on light surfaces */
  opacity: calc(.07 + var(--c) * .4);
}

/* state: set --c to var(--c-rest), var(--c-view) or var(--c-active) */

The symbol, rebuilt

The symbol is two cards. One is drawn as an outline and the other is solid. Where they overlap, the outline is cut away. Drag the slider to move the cards together and watch the cut appear.

+ =
  • Exclude keeps the parts of two shapes that don't overlap and removes the part that does. The outline disappears wherever it crosses the solid card.
  • In SVG that is one path with fill-rule="evenodd" holding three outlines: the outside of the ring, the inside of the ring and the solid card. Anything covered an even number of times is left empty. It works wherever the cards are, so they can move.
  • The numbers. Both cards are 264.4 by 177.2 with 20.6 corners. The outline grows outward by 5.73. The outline card tilts 19.4° to the left and the solid card 20.45° to the right.
  • Files. brands/symbol.svg is the still version. brands/symbol-animated.svg plays the cards coming together by itself, with no script, and has a dark preview background you can delete.

Using it

  • Dark grounds only. The file is white, so it sits on dark surfaces like everything else in this system. There's no dark version yet.
  • Leave room on the right. The full logo is about four times as wide as the symbol is tall, and it opens over whatever is next to it. Keep that space empty, as the top bar in the Layout section does.
  • Size. Keep the symbol at least 24px tall.
  • Leave it as drawn. Don't stretch it, recolor it or put texture on it.
  • Files. The original is brands/logo.svg. This page uses an inline copy so the name can animate, which a plain image tag can't do.
How it moves. The first time the logo scrolls into view, the two cards slide together over about a second. The name slides out from behind the symbol while a mask opens to the right, which takes about half a second. It closes the same way, a little faster. With reduced motion turned on it just switches. Like the texture, it runs on GSAP, so run npm install once. Without GSAP the full logo shows all the time.

Brand safety

CapsuleHotOrNot works with Steam data but is not a Steam product. The visual system keeps that line clear.

  • No Steam look-alike colors. Do not use #171a21, #1b2838, #2a475e or #66c0f4 anywhere in the UI.
  • Steam sign-in: use Valve's official button image, unmodified. Do not restyle it in our colors.
  • Reward cards: value and points first, brand name as plain Figtree text, on our own card template. No brand logos or artwork unless a supplier's terms allow it.
  • Game art: show only capsule art a developer uploaded or approved. Use placeholders in public mockups.
  • Footer: "Steam and the Steam logo are trademarks and/or registered trademarks of Valve Corporation in the U.S. and/or other countries. CapsuleHotOrNot is not affiliated with or endorsed by Valve Corporation."
  • Gift card brands: "Gift card brands are trademarks of their respective owners and are not sponsors of or affiliated with CapsuleHotOrNot."
